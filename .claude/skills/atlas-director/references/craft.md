# Craft: how a photo film works

General editing craft, for stills on a moving camera. The owner's own rules in taste.md win.

## What makes a cut good (in order of weight)

1. **Emotion**: does the next photo make the viewer feel the right next thing?
2. **Story**: does it move the film on (new place, new person, closer, the answer to the last shot)?
3. **Rhythm**: does it land at the right moment of the music?
4. **Eye trace**: where the eye is at the end of shot A, something worth looking at should be near
   there at the start of shot B. A subject on the right followed by a subject on the right cuts
   smoothly; right to far left jolts (use that only on purpose, on a hit).
5. **Screen direction**: keep things moving the same way across a run (a car driving right, then
   another driving right). Reverse it only at a turn of the story.

## Contrast carries the film

Cut between differences: wide to tight, dark to light, crowd to one face, still to moving, warm to
cold. Two similar photos in a row (same car, same angle) blur into one; keep the stronger.

## Moving the camera over a still

- **Every move goes toward something.** A push-in ends on the subject (eyes, grille, sign). A pan runs
  the way the subject faces or moves, or along the photo's leading lines, and stops on something. Never
  drift into empty sky, tarmac or the edge of the frame.
- **Push-in** = closer, tension, intimacy. **Pull-out** = reveal, release, context. **Drift** = calm
  breathing for SUPPORT shots. **Whip** = energy between two related shots, on a hit; never into a
  title, never twice in a row.
- **Speed matches the photo**: a strong photo moves slowly (it is enough); a weak one should not be
  in the film.
- **Never crop what matters**: heads, faces, hands doing the thing, the car's front, a sign's words,
  horizons (keep them level and off the exact middle).
- **Frame on thirds** for a single subject; centre only for symmetry or confrontation.

## Pace

- Hold HEROes for at least two beats; let one shot breathe in every five or six.
- Do not cut on every beat. Cut on the bar, on the snare, on a swell, and leave some beats empty.
- Speed up into the drop, then hold on the drop's shot. The fastest stretch is short.
- The first 1.5 s decide whether anyone stays: the best photo, already in motion.

## Title and text

Readable for at least 1.5 s, fully inside the frame at every moment it is seen, never landing by whip.
A title is a pause in the music, a breath, or the last frame; not the hook.

## Mood recipes (starting points, not rules)

- **Spooky / night**: slower than you think, start from black or near-black, reveal subjects out of
  the dark (pull-out or a slow push toward a face), off-centre framing and a slight drift for unease,
  hold a silence before the drop, fewer whips, cold or sodium-orange tones together, end on a face
  or a mask looking at us.
- **Lowriders / street culture**: chrome and paint up close, low angles, cars moving the same way
  across a run, hops and bounces on the snare, people with the cars (pride), the boulevard wide at
  the peak, end on one car rolling away or the crowd.
- **Ocean / calm**: long holds, horizontal pans along the horizon, cut on the wave's swell, few
  shots, an end that fades rather than cuts.
- **Film / black and white**: grain is texture, not a fault; slower cuts; strong graphic shapes;
  contrast cuts (white on black after black on white).
