# The owner's taste

Rules learned from the owner's feedback. They override craft.md. Add each new point with the date and
the video that taught it; never delete one without asking.

## Standing judgement (2026-11-19)

After two auto-edited reels (a lowrider parade, a Halloween night): "technically proficient at making the
content and using the tool, but missing taste, direction, attention to detail on what is in frame,
mistakes where the panning and zooming happens, and lack of touch."

What that meant in those two reels, as rules:

1. **Never let the auto-edit choose the photos.** Every shot is chosen from the casting sheet.
2. **Never open on a weak photo.** The Halloween reel opened on 1.5 s of a dark, soft arm.
3. **Look at what is in frame at a size where it can be judged** (540 px), at every cut and through
   every move. 180 px thumbnails hid soft focus and cut-off subjects.
4. **A cut-off title is a defect even mid-move.** Both reels had titles running off the frame edge.
5. **Deliver what was promised.** The "spooky spiral" layout was never seen whole.
6. **Touch: vary the holds.** Shots were nearly all 0.9 s; nothing was allowed to breathe.

## Films the owner loves (2026-11-19)

1. **A match-cut photo reel** (instagram.com/reel/CtxZkFfsite, by f0nzel, 29 s). Hundreds of photos in
   chapters of one subject (lamp posts, trucks, cyclists, window grids, ornaments, statues), each photo
   framed so the subject sits in exactly the same place and size, cut about every 0.1 s: one cyclist that
   keeps becoming someone else. Chapters change by shape (grid to grid, curve to curve). It ends on the
   lamp post it began with: a perfect loop.
2. **A scale journey** (instagram.com/reel/CusOhoeJBe-, by conner.griffith, 43 s). One round shape held
   dead centre the whole film while the scale grows: cell, egg, buttons, jellyfish, painted dome, crater
   lake, Mars, the sun, a nebula, a galaxy, a deep field. One direction of travel, start to end.
3. **Erol Alkan, "A Hold on Love"** (youtube.com/watch?v=zlCjrSy0-08, 5:52). The opposite speed: still,
   ordinary landscapes held for many seconds, no camera tricks, small subtitle lines telling a first-person
   story (a walk, the woods, a crowd dancing at decks, a sunrise) that ends on black: "That was my
   first time." The feeling comes from restraint and the words, not from the pictures moving.

What they share, as rules:

7. **One idea, held strictly.** Name the film's single device (same place, same shape, one journey, one
   story) and make every cut obey it. No mixing of devices to fill time.
8. **Every cut has a reason you can say:** the subject lands where the last one was, the shape
   continues, or the story's next line arrives. A cut without one is removed.
9. **No effects.** Waves, jiggles, pulses, shakes and whips are not in any of them. The photos and the
   cut do the work. Use a motion only if it is the idea.
10. **Fast or still, never in between.** Either rapid runs (0.1 to 0.3 s a photo) with the subject
    locked, or long holds (3 s and more). The 0.9 s every-shot rhythm is the mark of the auto-edit.
11. **Consistency is the craft.** Same position, same size, same horizon line across a run: worth
    measuring each frame (`subject`, `frame`), because a jump of a few percent breaks the spell.
12. **End by returning or by resolving.** Loop to the first frame, or go to black with one quiet line.
13. **Text is small and human**, a line someone would say, not a big title card.

## Notes on the "Masks" Halloween reel (2026-11-19)

"Too zoomed in and can't see most of the photos. I'm trying to showcase the photos and then zoom in on
specific subjects for dramatic effect. The pacing here was better but I want it to be more dynamic, with
motion in appropriate moments that matches the song."

14. **Showcase first, then drama.** A photo is first seen whole (`room`), and only then, on a musical
    moment, does the camera push to its subject. A crop that hides most of the photo is not the default.
15. **The owner's favourite form:** all photos spread evenly in one arrangement; the camera goes into one
    photo, shows it whole, pushes to its subject for drama, pulls back out, travels across the layout
    surgically (one clean eased move, landing exactly) to the next photo and goes back in.
16. **Motion lands on the music:** pushes and pulls land on beats, the big moves on swells and the drop;
    stillness in the quiet. Still, cut-only reels are too static for the owner.
17. **Sound effects from `audio/sfx/`, tastefully:** a few, on the big moves (a whoosh under a travel, a
    hit under the drop push), never under every shot.

## Notes on the first "Showcase" wall (2026-11-19)

"Make it look like the images extend in all directions forever; it's okay to repeat them as long as they
are not right next to each other. And don't use the same zoom-in effect only: you can zoom in to the left
side of a horizontal photo and then pan across it so the height of the photo takes up the entire height
of the screen, and so on for vertical."

18. **The wall never ends on screen.** Grow the layout with copies (`extend`) so no edge, title block or
    empty black is ever seen; a copy is never next to its own photo.
19. **Vary the move to the photo:** a horizontal photo fills the screen's height and pans across it
    (`align` start then end, arrival scroll); a vertical one fills the width and tilts; the showcase
    (whole photo) and the push to a subject are two more. Never the same move twice in a row.

## Notes on the "Showcase" reel (2026-11-19)

"The sound effects need to be turned down to like 30-40 percent. The fish bike thing is not a good
thing to zoom into like that, pick a better shot."

20. **Sound effects sit under the song:** volume 0.3 to 0.4, never louder.
21. **A dramatic push needs a subject that earns it:** a face, eyes, a gesture; ideally looking at us.
    An object or a costume prop (the fish-mouth bike float) is shown, not dived into.

## Notes on workspace composition (2026-11-26)

"Make sure to group photos according to what they are — for example the low riders nested workspace should
have a group for the actual shots used in the scene arranged so that they are layered correctly onto one
another, and the other photos from the tag should be grouped together and labelled as such."

22. **Group the workspace by role:** put the photos used in the scene into a labelled locked group (e.g.
    `Nested Scene`) stacked in exact top-to-bottom order so clicking any layer never buries the others,
    and group the remaining unused photos from the tag into their own labelled group (e.g.
    `Unused Tag Photos`).
