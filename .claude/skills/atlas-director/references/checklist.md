# Review checklist

Go through every item against real pictures (`cuts`, `picture` at 540 px), not the timeline. A "no" is
fixed and pictured again.

## Opening and ending
- [ ] The first frame is a HERO, sharp, lit, already in motion. Not a title, a grid, a dark or soft photo.
- [ ] By 1.5 s the viewer knows what this film is about.
- [ ] The last frame answers the opening and holds long enough to land (and loops cleanly if it is a loop).

## Every frame
- [ ] No head, face, hand, car front, sign text or horizon cut by the frame edge.
- [ ] Horizons level.
- [ ] Nothing soft, near-black or duplicated that the casting sheet marked CUT.
- [ ] No empty space (gap between photos, background) filling more than a sliver, unless on purpose.

## Every move
- [ ] Each push-in ends on the subject, each pull-out reveals something worth revealing.
- [ ] Each pan runs with the subject or the lines and stops on something.
- [ ] No move ends between photos or on an edge.
- [ ] Whips only between related photos, on a hit, never into text, never twice in a row.

## Every cut
- [ ] Eye trace: the eye can find the next subject near where it was.
- [ ] Contrast with the shot before (size, light, subject); no two near-identical shots in a row.
- [ ] Screen direction kept across the run, or broken on purpose.
- [ ] Lands on a beat, bar, snare or swell that means something; not every beat.

## Pace and arc
- [ ] Shot lengths vary; HEROes hold two beats or more; one shot breathes in every five or six.
- [ ] The build gets faster and closer, the drop holds, the release slows.
- [ ] The best two or three photos are at the peak, not used up early.
- [ ] If the layout was the idea, it is seen whole at least once, at a moment that earns it.

## Text
- [ ] Every title fully inside the frame for its whole stay, readable for at least 1.5 s.
- [ ] Spelling and dates right.

## Sound
- [ ] The song window starts with intent (not mid-phrase) and the film ends on a phrase end or fades.
- [ ] `timeline` warnings: each fixed or written down with why.
